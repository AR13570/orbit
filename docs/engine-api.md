# Orbit engine contract

This contract describes Odessey generation, gravity, progression, debug navigation and flight behavior.

## Off-screen flight and countdown contract

`OFFSCREEN_TIMEOUT_SECONDS=6` is exported from physics.ts. Projectiles retain full body/goal gravity and swept collisions/docking outside the actual visible rectangle. Crossing an edge never clips or kills a projectile. A physical circle touching or intersecting the rectangle, including its rounded corner contact, counts as visible. Only a fully outside circle starts the timeout.

`Particle.offscreenRemaining?:number` expresses active fixed-step simulation seconds until that piece expires. It is defined only while fully outside, omitted while visible, and zero at timeout. Solid collision or docking outside can leave a positive value on the now-inactive particle, so feedback must consider live particles. Snapshot copies remain detached. Initial prediction follows the same physics and continuous visibility accounting without mutating live state. The timeout contact position is resolved within a fixed step, just like swept solid/goal contacts.

Each piece keeps its own continuous outside elapsed time. Actual flight re-entry, including a complete visibility pass between fixed-step endpoints, resets that piece. Re-entry caused by camera/resize also resets it. Repeated `setFlightBounds` calls preserve elapsed while still outside, including identical per-frame injections and changed rectangles. Resizing a previously visible piece outside grants six active seconds, without indefinite arming grace. Branch pieces inherit the center's existing elapsed when created outside, preventing extra time by tapping. One expired piece leaves the others alive; only all-lost fails the volley. Pause freezes time by withholding updates. There is no overall on-screen lifetime and retries remain unlimited. Generator challenge probes deliberately have no visibility death and are unchanged.

## Procedural architecture and acceptance

The generator uses twenty structural grammars in `structures.ts`: staggered binary, three-step slalom, twin-well entrance, forked approach, split exit, alternating corridor, diagonal ladder, crescent basin, horseshoe pocket, unequal binary, separated clusters, opposing banks, outer hairpin, offset paired gates, asymmetric fan, stepped shelves, triangular islands, sparse anchors, repellent catch and chained binaries. A grammar determines grouping, ordering, gaps, occupied regions and the goal relation. It is not merely a label added after generation.

The slalom has three paired gates with alternating opening centers. Clusters have two compact four-body diamond islands and a body-free slab at least 150 units wide. Shelves have three horizontal pairs progressing diagonally. These intentionally open structures use sampled dynamics rather than inserting the same central blocker into every layout. The former proposed open hourglass was rejected because dense tests found shallow captures; the playable two-offset-gate arrangement is honestly called offset paired gates. Other families retain their distinct slot arrangements, with additional gate, ladder, bank, triangular-area and goal-pocket predicates. `matchesStructure` checks defining ordering and shape bounds for normal generation, perturbed backups and exact certificates.

`generateSector(seed,index=0,launch?,recent=[])` mixes the run seed and checkpoint index through a 32-bit PRNG. A seeded shuffle of all twenty grammars controls each twenty-field cycle. The selected family must have a fresh usable certificate before construction starts. Randomness changes group spacing, relative vertical span, goal relation, mass and eligible gravity polarities, with reflection as an additional variation rather than a new family. Recent family identity is explicitly excluded alongside geometric similarity. Reproduction uses the same seed, index and recent descriptors. Math.random is used only when OrbitEngine chooses a fresh run seed.

Odessey difficulty advances with completed checkpoints and reaches its maximum at level 15. `odesseyDifficulty(index)` uses zero-based sector indices; the profile is capped after index 14. `generation.difficulty` publishes the normalized factor (0, 1/3, 2/3 or 1). Retry keeps the field and its difficulty; restart begins at the opening stage.

Generation continues indefinitely after level 15 with the capped profile. Extra solids are added to the selected family's core. Gravity scales independently of the fixed 45-unit docking radius and player launch power. Validated routes and four nearby shots must dock within canonical world bounds in 22 simulation seconds. Angle and power tolerances apply separately, with power clamped to the player's 0 to 1 range. Minimum capture-chord excursions are 70, 80, 90 and 100 units respectively.

`progressive-layouts.ts` contains 28 offline screened courses: eight families at stage 1, eleven at stage 2 and nine at stage 3. Runtime selects seeded families and exact reflections, then translates them to the next launch position. It does not jitter these advanced courses, because that could reopen a shortcut. Original opening generation retains all twenty grammars, bounded proposal searches and family-specific fallback certificates. Advanced generation replays the five routes, shape/clearance checks, goal-facing collisions and force-removal evidence before publishing a course.

`analyzeGravityBends` groups actual trajectory heading changes into alternating turns. Each qualifying turn must exceed 0.16 radians and cover at least 40 times the gravity scale in distance. A body's perpendicular force must exceed the goal's contribution, and successive counted bends must be dominated by distinct bodies. Tiny oscillations and goal-only curves do not count. Every advanced witness also requires at least two distinct bend bodies whose individual gravity removal causes an actual collision while their solid surfaces and every other force remain active. An unresolved trajectory never counts as positive difficulty evidence.

`screenGravityShortcuts` independently checks 252 angles around the full circle and 41 powers from 0 to 1, observing every shot for 22 seconds. Any captured trajectory with fewer than the required bends rejects a course. This is finite empirical screening, not a proof about every continuous angle and power. `npm run verify:odessey` verifies the immutable inputs in `scripts/odessey-multibend-sources.json`; adding `-- --write` republishes the runtime data only after all checks pass. The report is written to `/private/tmp/orbit-odessey-certificate-verification.json`.

Each stage has more than six pairwise fresh families. Runtime freshness still excludes the previous six families and similar silhouettes. Reflections may obscure only their own family's canonical certificate, preserving a future backup. A reflection is variation within a family and is never relabeled as a new structure.

## Debug level navigation and zoom

Open `/?mode=odessey&debug=1&level=15&seed=42` to start at level 15. `debug=true` is also accepted, and enabled debug opens Odessey when mode is omitted. Explicit Gravity or Creator selection takes precedence. The level is one-based and must be a positive safe integer; malformed values return to one. A level parameter without enabled debug is ignored. Debug skips automatic first-time help, shows the active level, preserves that start level on New seed and permits normal progression beyond it.

`new OrbitEngine(seed?, startLevel=1)` and `restart(seed?, startLevel=1)` expose the same level jump. Starts through level 20 reconstruct ordinary seed history exactly. Higher debug jumps reconstruct only the six preceding fields, keeping the work bounded; they are deterministic but can differ from a complete journey with that seed. The checkpoint counter starts at level minus one. Retry retains the field.

World size is independent of the screen. The default camera fits the course, including source and goal; gameplay zoom ranges from 25% to 400%. Reset zoom restores the whole-course fit. Zoom changes visibility and the existing off-screen timer, not gravity or collision geometry.

## Curvature and truthful validation modes

Every generated sector publishes `generation.validation` as `geometric` or `sampled`, alongside the actual `family` and `fallback` flag. The property is optional in the shared type only to support older hand-built fixtures; generated sectors always supply it. `MIN_CAPTURE_CHORD_BEND=70` and its legacy alias `MIN_TRANSFER_DETOUR=70` are generation criteria. The former 80-unit capture-chord and 100-unit goal-centerline claims no longer apply. Live physics do not enforce artificial route gates.

For geometric fields, `blocksStraightCorridor(layout)` proves at least 70 units of bend relative to every actual launch-to-capture-contact chord, regardless of launch power or angle. Let D be launch-to-goal-center distance, R the capture aperture radius (goal plus ship, currently 50), and B=70. All possible contact chords lie within the tangent cone of half-angle asin(R/D). Each solid's collision disc is eroded by B. Its angular shadow contributes an interval of chord directions that pass within the eroded disc. The union of these intervals must cover the entire closed capture cone exactly, including its tangents. Multiple different bodies may cover different portions of the cone; no universal dominant circle is required.

A contributing body's projected blocking plane must be after launch and before all capture contacts: its minimum cone projection is positive and its center distance is less than D-R. Bodies inside or behind the capture aperture cannot certify a shortcut obstruction. A launch inside any solid is invalid. Intervals merge without an epsilon that bridges a real gap. At the relevant cross-section, a surviving path must avoid the full collision disc, forcing lateral departure of at least B from its capture chord. The proof concerns capture-chord bend, not a separate 100-unit centerline excursion.

Every field, including sampled fields, also requires positive collision evidence: all 25 high-power goal-facing shots (powers .8, .85, .9, .95, 1 and offsets 0, plus/minus .025, plus/minus .05 radians) collide with solids. Removing body forces from the ordinary solution, while retaining the strong goal force and original solid collision circles, must also produce a collision. An unresolved shot never counts as a collision or evidence of difficulty.

For sampled fields, all five tolerant solutions additionally have measured capture-chord bend at least 70. Perturbations first screen a base grid of 651 inputs: powers 0 through 1 in .05 steps and goal-bearing offsets -.75 through .75 radians in .05 steps, observing actual physics for eight seconds times the formation gravity scale, capped at 22 seconds per shot. Captured regions with bend below 110 receive an adaptive neighborhood refinement at .025 power and .025 radians. Results are cached, so the bounded screen visits at most the 2,501 distinct points of that finer grid. Any sampled capture below 70 rejects the perturbation. Budget exhaustion rejects the whole candidate. A fully observed unresolved wide-grid shot is recorded as inconclusive rather than falsely counted as a collision. This is finite empirical screening, not a continuous guarantee or proof for untested inputs. Independently finer, longer 22-second offline sweeps check weak powers, intermediate angles/powers and the anchors and representative generated fields. New counterexamples should become regressions.

`probeFlight`, `acceptsChallenge`, `acceptsCoreChallenge`, `acceptsSampledCurvature`, `captureChordBend`, the challenge constants and `SearchBudget` are exported from challenge.ts. Probe paths include exact swept docking contact, so bend is measured against the true contact chord. Probes omit visible-edge death. The default observation cap is 22 seconds; the explicit runtime wide screen uses eight. Swept solid contact at or before docking has collision priority. An axis-aligned circle-box rejection skips quadratic collision work only for segments that cannot touch; all possible contacts retain the exact quadratic. Player forces, launch speed, collision sizes, branching and off-screen timers are unchanged.

## Structural anti-repetition

`CompositionDescriptor`, `describeComposition`, `compositionDistance`, `geometryDistance`, `freshComposition` and `preservesFallbackCoverage` are exported from composition.ts. Descriptors quantize body positions to 80-unit bins and radius to 16-unit bins relative to launch, plus coarse goal position. They exclude IDs, seed and index. Kind/goal changes cannot disguise identical obstacle geometry.

Freshness rejects the same family in the last six fields and requires BOTH combined composition distance at least 1.2 and body-geometry/size distance at least 0.75 against each of the last six accepted fields. Geometry compares bidirectional nearest occupied cells and sizes, with a count penalty; combined distance additionally compares kinds and goal. This rejects small coordinate jitter and familiar silhouettes while preserving genuine composition changes.

OrbitEngine holds only six recent descriptors. Advance records the new accepted layout; retry leaves history alone; restart clears it. Replay of the same run seed reproduces the sequence, including after retries. No previous-level recursion or unbounded cache exists. Standalone generator calls may supply the same optional recent window.

Acceptance also maintains certified fallback coverage. If the resulting six-entry history contains c certified layouts, it must leave at least 7-c fresh certificates available. A normal layout is rejected if it would destroy this reserve. Because certificates are pairwise fresh, choosing one consumes only itself; dropping an old certificate reopens it. This preserves a safe fresh choice through successive emergency selections for valid run history, instead of hoping another random search succeeds. Certificate validity, pairwise freshness, coverage-preserving generation and consecutive guaranteed selections are regression requirements.

## World geometry and shared schema

Canonical bounds use the difficulty profile's width and height, with origin `{x:launch.x-100,y:launch.y-height/2}`. Initial launch is `{x:120,y:500}`. Subsequent launch equals the former goal center exactly. Goal distance follows the stage table above and stays at most 200 times the formation scale in Y units from the launch centerline. Runtime coordinates remain global; construction works locally and replays the chosen flight globally for exact metadata arithmetic.

Body radius controls collision and the gravitational mass term: `bodyStrength(radius)=radius²*900`. Long-field `Body.gravityScale` scales minimum force softening and divides acceleration by the formation scale, allowing longer travel at the same launch power. Solid surfaces remain at least 50 units apart at levels 5 to 9 and 65 units apart otherwise, launch clearance at least 65 units, and goal-to-solid surface clearance at least 30 units. Generated goal radius remains 45; ship radius remains 5, making true docking contact 50 units from goal center. No score, lives, shields, powers, pickups or inert rubble exist.

`Sector` retains seed/index, width/height, bounds, generation family/fallback/validation, launch, checkpoint, bodies and test-only solution metadata. `solution` contains actual validated angle, power, speed, flightSeconds and path. Speed equals launchSpeed(power); fixed 240 is no longer a generation requirement. Paths sample every four fixed steps plus the clipped capture point. Time counts completed fixed steps exactly as live elapsed does. Metadata is never rendered as the answer for players. Default aim retains its angle/power offset from the valid solution and now uses that solution's variable power.

`GameSnapshot` retains status, sector, aim, particles, checkpointCount, message, elapsed, event, shotCount and canBranch. Status is `aiming | flying | docked | failed`. Static sector snapshots are detached, recursively frozen and cached; mutable aim, particles and trails are copied independently.

## Independent strong goal attraction

The goal is a special non-colliding inward well whose pull is independent of its small visual/capture radius. Constants are `CHECKPOINT_ASSIST_STRENGTH=1_000_000_000`, `CHECKPOINT_SOFTENING=90`, `CHECKPOINT_ASSIST_RANGE=800` and `CHECKPOINT_MAX_ACCELERATION=1200`. Softened inward acceleration smoothly saturates and has a squared finite-range falloff. Exact center is finite zero. Without a formation scale, the combined body-plus-goal force caps actual acceleration at 840. Long fields supply `Checkpoint.gravityScale`: attraction range and softening multiply by the scale, the mass term multiplies by its square, final acceleration divides by it, and the combined acceleration cap becomes 840 divided by the scale. This preserves the formation dynamics over a longer journey without requiring launch power above one. Invalid or omitted scales use one; docking contact remains independent of this scale.

`checkpointForceAt(position,checkpoint)` exposes the goal contribution. `forceAt(position,bodies,checkpoint?)` and `stepParticle(position,velocity,bodies,dt=FIXED_STEP,checkpoint?)` preserve legacy no-goal calls; optional checkpoint is the fifth stepParticle argument. Valid goal size does not scale its force: radius 45 and radius 90 produce the same acceleration. Goal radius still determines only actual capture contact.

Live movement, prediction, all branched pieces, procedural validation and fallback verification use this same physics. No teleport, snap, damping or docking outside the true boundary is introduced. Swept solid collision has priority over docking at the same/earlier contact. At distance 100, helper acceleration is 1129.78 and actual capped acceleration 840. In the fixture start (100,500), velocity (250,0), goal (400,640), radius 45, the unassisted 140-unit miss is captured at 0.6531 seconds, live elapsed 0.6583. A 160-unit miss in the same fixture remains outside capture. This supersedes the previous 80-unit rescue evidence.

## Engine lifecycle, split control and visible bounds

OrbitEngine supports constructor(seed?, startLevel=1), getSnapshot, setAim, launch, update, retry, advance, restart, predict and setFlightBounds. Fixed step is 1/120 second, maximum input frame delta 0.25 seconds, trails cap 180 points. Prediction caps at 22 seconds; live flight has no overall lifetime. Solid collisions and six continuous outside seconds permit unlimited retries at the same checkpoint. Retry while flying aborts; only restart resets checkpoint progress. Docking increments checkpointCount once and advance is accepted once from docked.

Aiming-only setShotCount(1|3) and toggleShotCount select the mode; selection survives retries, advance and restart. Both modes initially launch and predict ONE center projectile. In split mode, branch() works once during flight while canBranch is true: center continues untouched and two pieces start at its current position with current velocity rotated plus/minus three degrees. Side trails start at that split point. No hypothetical future branch is predicted. Pieces independently experience all forces, collisions and outside timers; the earliest docking ends the volley once, and failure requires every piece to be lost.

`setFlightBounds(bounds|null)` receives the renderer's actual visible world rectangle. Null restores canonical defaults; invalid/nonfinite or too-small rectangles are ignored. The engine copies the rectangle and preserves it across restart/advance. Visibility uses intersection of the physical circle and rectangle. Each live piece retains its continuous outside elapsed across bounds updates, resets on actual visibility re-entry and expires after six outside seconds. Prediction and live timeout positions agree. Camera framing, controls, pause, feedback timing, sound and persistence remain integration responsibilities.
