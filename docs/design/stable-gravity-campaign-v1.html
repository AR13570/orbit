A new direction for Gravity

Orbits worth keeping.

Build a little solar system, one planet at a time. Thirty original layout proposals, with room to think between launches.

Review sketches, not implemented levels. The curves show intended orbit shapes, not simulated or proven solutions. The current campaign is unchanged. I recommend starting with the first 24 and validating the six stretch candidates separately.
Full orbits, then lasting orbits

At least two whole revolutions at each required target. After the last launch, every required planet must remain in a bounded orbit for at least 120 simulated seconds and ten full laps. A flyby never qualifies. Relay designs stay out of the first 24; any future relay must make a full orbit at every stop and end in a proven stable orbit.

One launch at a time

Solutions must allow at least four seconds of real time between launches, plus longer thinking pauses. Test delayed launches, small aiming errors and different launch orders. No synchronized volleys.

Keep what you already built

Adding the next planet must preserve earlier orbits with Particle Interaction enabled and grouping off. Check portrait, landscape and both projectile-size extremes before calling a level playable.

More breathing room.
No letter labels.

The core face contains only the force symbol and its orbit count. A proposed 64px default diameter, with a 52px minimum at wider views, leaves space around both. Repellers show their outward symbol without an irrelevant counter.

Gravity strength does not need to increase with the face size. During implementation, orbit and collision clearance must be checked against the larger visible edge. The world layout, not a shrinking symbol, should adapt to a small screen.

Why the campaign needs a rebuild.

I replayed all 45 existing saved solutions, then continued the simulation for up to 120 seconds after completion. Only 13 kept the objective met and every finishing projectile alive. Seventeen require gaps under 2.5 real seconds between some launches.

This tests the existing saved solutions, not every possible trajectory. A failed replay does not prove that a level has no better solution. The test used the authored default radius, particle gravity enabled, and grouping off; it is narrower than the proposed release criteria above.
