# Gravity campaign: critical variety and pacing review

Reviewed 18 October 2026, after the campaign reorder and the Deep softening change to 200.

**Verdict: accepted with limits within the requested 45-level, nine-base campaign, where population variants are allowed.** The two important repetition findings from the first review are resolved. Shield and Crown remain gentle practice variations; that is a design limitation to describe honestly, not a remaining blocker under this scope.

This assessment examines the current runtime definitions, canonical launch sequences, numerical trajectories and progression. It does not claim that solver success proves the game is fun, or that a reviewer manually played every level. Browser input and long survival verification are complementary checks recorded separately.

## Findings and corrections

| Earlier finding | Current result |
| --- | --- |
| Seven consecutive Deep single-planet levels repeated the same spatial paths faster. | The sequence interleaves these variants with different objectives. Deep uses softening200, versus Gentle180, so its spatial paths and precession also differ. |

The new order uses shared-orbit returns as breathing spaces among higher-population local challenges. Population does not rise strictly at every step. This is intentional pacing rather than a claim that every later level must be harder than the last.

There are still 33 single-core entries among the 45 levels. Population two and three add timing and particle interaction, but they do not create new base geometry. The current progression makes that repetition more deliberate and avoids the former uninterrupted ending. If human playtesting still reports sameness, Shield and Crown are the first families to reduce or redesign.

## Evidence for the changed Deep fields

To distinguish spatial variation from faster playback, I compared Gentle and Deep at equal accumulated orbital angle over their first two revolutions. Maximum radial differences were 5.23 to 7.79 world units for the five single-core bases, 20.40 for Common centre, and 18.92 for Broken necklace. Open sky's third periapsis shifted from approximately 10.968 to 10.714 radians. These are real changes to spatial precession, with modest variation in the local loops and larger variation in shared orbits.

Earlier force-ablation probes found Shield and Crown's canonical net repeller acceleration averaged below one percent of attractive acceleration. An unchanged introductory central-orbit shot also solved those fields. Successful transfer is useful teaching, but it is why this review calls them gentle variants rather than distinct strategic mechanics. The revision does not depend on pretending that this limitation vanished.
