# Orbit engine contract

Source and focused tests are frozen. This contract includes the difficulty, off-screen-flight and capture-aperture refinements. Engine ownership is `src/game/**`, `tests/engine/**` and this document. Visual owns the renderer. Integration owns application code and E2E fixtures. Main owns application-wide final unit, build and browser verification.

## Off-screen flight and countdown contract

`OFFSCREEN_TIMEOUT_SECONDS=6` is exported from physics.ts. Projectiles retain full body/goal gravity and swept collisions/docking outside the actual visible rectangle. Crossing an edge never clips or kills a projectile. A physical circle touching or intersecting the rectangle, including its rounded corner contact, counts as visible. Only a fully outside circle starts the timeout.

`Particle.offscreenRemaining?:number` expresses active fixed-step simulation seconds until that piece expires. It is defined only while fully outside, omitted while visible, and zero at timeout. Solid collision or docking outside can leave a positive value on the now-inactive particle, so feedback must consider live particles. Snapshot copies remain detached. Initial prediction follows the same physics and continuous visibility accounting without mutating live state. The timeout contact position is resolved within a fixed step, just like swept solid/goal contacts.

Each piece keeps its own continuous outside elapsed time. Actual flight re-entry, including a complete visibility pass between fixed-step endpoints, resets that piece. Re-entry caused by camera/resize also resets it. Repeated `setFlightBounds` calls preserve elapsed while still outside, including identical per-frame injections and changed rectangles. Resizing a previously visible piece outside grants six active seconds, without indefinite arming grace. Branch pieces inherit the center's existing elapsed when created outside, preventing extra time by tapping. One expired piece leaves the others alive; only all-lost fails the volley. Pause freezes time by withholding updates. There is no overall on-screen lifetime and retries remain unlimited. Generator challenge probes deliberately have no visibility death and are unchanged.

## Procedural architecture and acceptance

Every field contains a substantial attractive solid across the actual launch-to-goal chord, including its entire capture aperture. Its X position, Y position and radius vary. Four to seven mixed attractive/repellent solids form seeded spatial clusters around the forward corridor. There is no reserved empty center band, cycling positional family template or fixed satellite row.

`generateSector(seed,index=0,launch?,recent=[])` mixes run seed and checkpoint index through a 32-bit PRNG. Randomness controls count, goal, barrier geometry, surrounding positions/radii/kinds and route-search preference. Reproduction uses the same seed, index and recent descriptors. Math.random is used only when OrbitEngine chooses a fresh run seed.

Goals remain 660 to 820 units forward and at most 120 Y units from launch. The chord blocker samples X 280 to 480, Y on the actual chord plus/minus six units, and radius 110 to 140 (110 to 122 in random backup construction). Its placement must meet existing launch, solid and goal clearances and the analytic certificate below before expensive route search begins. The mandatory blocker is attractive: the larger repellent barrier did not yield tolerant on-screen neighborhoods in controlled exploration under unchanged forces. Surrounding repellent solids retain varied position, radius, count and influence. This is an intentional change from the previous smaller central body whose kind varied.

The pipeline finds an ordinary-power assisted route around that solid, using powers 0.52, 0.60 and 0.75. It then traces the same shot with obstacle forces removed and places a dependence blocker where needed. It similarly places up to two blockers against surviving cheap goal-facing trajectories. Blocker positions come from actual traces. Every added solid must preserve the central route and four axis neighbors under the full combined force. Remaining clustered additions preserve those same five routes. Final acceptance rechecks all geometry and trajectory criteria below. Family labels classify arrangements/routes; they do not select positions.

Search remains bounded: at most 12 main layout attempts and 90 spatial proposals per attempt. Initial guide search explores 21 angle magnitudes on both sides and the three ordinary powers, prioritizing larger bends; each candidate gets at most 8,000 guide-search physics steps. This prevents one unproductive layout from consuming the entire run budget. Main construction retains a shared 60,000-step budget; the independent random backup retains 30,000 steps and 12 attempts. Positive route validation caps at six seconds. Each targeted blocker gets 28 proposals. Challenge probes cap at 22 seconds and reject unresolved or depleted work. These are generation budgets, not live flight limits.

`generateFallbackSector` uses the same analytic aperture, trajectory, tolerance and freshness requirements with smaller surrounding wells and four to six objects. If bounded random searches exhaust, `generateGuaranteedFallbackSector` selects from 20 independently recertified, mutually distinct populated layouts. Every certificate meets the current analytic guarantee and all actual trajectory checks. The reserve invariant documented below remains unchanged; neither freshness nor geometry is bypassed in emergencies.

## Difficulty acceptance and complete capture aperture

The previous 25 probes covered only powers 0.8 to 1 and angles within 0.05 radians of goal bearing. A wider investigation reproduced thousands of easy captures just beyond that cone and at lower powers. In seed 42 opening, power 0.975 at bearing minus 0.075 docked with only 10.754 units of deviation from the goal-center line. The ordinary power 0.4 at bearing minus 0.325 was also a bypass. Across 18 fields from six seeds and three successive checkpoints, the initial dense sweep found 3,582 captures deviating less than 65 units. Probe count alone did not establish meaningful route shape.

`blocksStraightCorridor(layout)` now proves a continuous geometric obstruction, independent of discrete aim/power samples. `MIN_TRANSFER_DETOUR=100` and `MIN_CAPTURE_CHORD_BEND=80` are generation criteria only; live physics never enforce an artificial gate.

Let D be launch-to-goal-center distance, R the actual capture aperture radius (goal radius plus ship radius, currently 50), a a solid's projected distance along the goal-center line, b its absolute perpendicular offset, and r its collision radius including ship radius. All possible launch-to-capture-contact chords lie inside the tangent cone with sin(alpha)=R/D. The solid's worst offset from any such chord is `b*cos(alpha)+a*sin(alpha)`. Certification requires both `r-b >= 100` and `r-(b*cos(alpha)+a*sin(alpha)) >= 80`. It also places the relevant cross-section strictly after launch and before every possible contact: `a*cos(alpha)-b*sin(alpha)>0` and `hypot(a,b)<D-R`, with launch clearance. Any continuous surviving trajectory reaching any point of the aperture must cross that solid's projected cross-section and therefore bend at least 80 units away from its actual launch-to-contact chord. A grazing capture cannot invalidate the guarantee.

Final acceptance also requires:

- The ordinary-power solution and four axis neighbors (angle plus/minus 0.025 and power plus/minus 0.025) dock under shared actual physics.
- The existing 25 high-power goal-facing probes all positively collide.
- The successful ordinary shot with obstacle forces removed, retaining every original solid and the full strong goal force, positively collides. The obstacle gravity must change the outcome.
- Composition freshness and emergency coverage still hold.

`probeFlight`, `acceptsChallenge`, `blocksStraightCorridor`, the challenge and detour constants and SearchBudget are exported from challenge.ts. Sampled probe paths now include the exact swept docking contact so actual capture-chord bend can be measured honestly. Probes omit visible-edge termination entirely. Body forces are disabled only during force evaluation; collision evaluation retains the original circles. Swept solid contact at or before docking has collision priority. Player launch speed, aim, goal attraction, collision sizes, branching, off-screen timers and unlimited retries are unchanged. The aperture certificate protects all continuous launch inputs. Broader dense trajectory confirmation belongs to focused regressions/recorded measurements rather than adding redundant runtime simulation for each constructed field. An axis-aligned circle-box rejection skips quadratic contact work only for segments that cannot touch; possible contacts retain the exact existing quadratic and priority.

## Structural anti-repetition

`CompositionDescriptor`, `describeComposition`, `compositionDistance`, `geometryDistance`, `freshComposition` and `preservesFallbackCoverage` are exported from composition.ts. Descriptors quantize body positions to 80-unit bins and radius to 16-unit bins relative to launch, plus coarse goal position. They exclude IDs, seed and index. Kind/goal changes cannot disguise identical obstacle geometry.

Freshness requires BOTH combined composition distance at least 1.2 and body-geometry/size distance at least 0.75 against each of the last six accepted fields. Geometry compares bidirectional nearest occupied cells and sizes, with a count penalty; combined distance additionally compares kinds and goal. This rejects small coordinate jitter and familiar silhouettes while preserving genuine composition changes.

OrbitEngine holds only six recent descriptors. Advance records the new accepted layout; retry leaves history alone; restart clears it. Replay of the same run seed reproduces the sequence, including after retries. No previous-level recursion or unbounded cache exists. Standalone generator calls may supply the same optional recent window.

Acceptance also maintains certified fallback coverage. If the resulting six-entry history contains c certified layouts, it must leave at least 7-c fresh certificates available. A normal layout is rejected if it would destroy this reserve. Because certificates are pairwise fresh, choosing one consumes only itself; dropping an old certificate reopens it. This preserves a safe fresh choice through successive emergency selections for valid run history, instead of hoping another random search succeeds. Certificate validity, pairwise freshness, coverage-preserving generation and consecutive guaranteed selections are regression requirements.

## World geometry and shared schema

Canonical bounds remain 1250 by 900 world units, with origin `{x:launch.x-100,y:launch.y-450}`. Initial launch is `{x:120,y:500}`. Subsequent launch equals the former goal center exactly. Generated goals are 660 to 820 X units forward and at most 120 Y units from the launch centerline. Runtime coordinates remain global; construction works locally and replays the chosen flight globally for exact metadata arithmetic.

Body radius honestly controls collision and ordinary gravitational mass: `bodyStrength(radius)=radius²*900`. Solid surfaces remain at least 65 units apart, launch clearance at least 65 units, and goal-to-solid surface clearance at least 30 units. Generated goal radius remains 45; ship radius remains 5, making true docking contact 50 units from goal center. No score, lives, shields, powers, pickups or inert rubble exist.

`Sector` retains seed/index, width/height, bounds, generation family/fallback, launch, checkpoint, bodies and test-only solution metadata. `solution` contains actual validated angle, power, speed, flightSeconds and path. Speed equals launchSpeed(power); fixed 240 is no longer a generation requirement. Paths sample every four fixed steps plus the clipped capture point. Time counts completed fixed steps exactly as live elapsed does. Metadata is never rendered as the answer for players. Default aim retains its angle/power offset from the valid solution and now uses that solution's variable power.

`GameSnapshot` retains status, sector, aim, particles, checkpointCount, message, elapsed, event, shotCount and canBranch. Status is `aiming | flying | docked | failed`. Static sector snapshots are detached, recursively frozen and cached; mutable aim, particles and trails are copied independently.

## Independent strong goal attraction

The goal is a special non-colliding inward well whose pull is independent of its small visual/capture radius. Constants are `CHECKPOINT_ASSIST_STRENGTH=1_000_000_000`, `CHECKPOINT_SOFTENING=90`, `CHECKPOINT_ASSIST_RANGE=800` and `CHECKPOINT_MAX_ACCELERATION=1200`. Softened inward acceleration smoothly saturates and has a squared finite-range falloff. Exact center is finite zero. The combined body-plus-goal force still caps actual acceleration at 840.

`checkpointForceAt(position,checkpoint)` exposes the goal contribution. `forceAt(position,bodies,checkpoint?)` and `stepParticle(position,velocity,bodies,dt=FIXED_STEP,checkpoint?)` preserve legacy no-goal calls; optional checkpoint is the fifth stepParticle argument. Valid goal size does not scale its force: radius 45 and radius 90 produce the same acceleration. Goal radius still determines only actual capture contact.

Live movement, prediction, all branched pieces, procedural validation and fallback verification use this same physics. No teleport, snap, damping or docking outside the true boundary is introduced. Swept solid collision has priority over docking at the same/earlier contact. At distance 100, helper acceleration is 1129.78 and actual capped acceleration 840. In the fixture start (100,500), velocity (250,0), goal (400,640), radius 45, the unassisted 140-unit miss is captured at 0.6531 seconds, live elapsed 0.6583. A 160-unit miss in the same fixture remains outside capture. This supersedes the previous 80-unit rescue evidence.

## Engine lifecycle, split control and visible bounds

OrbitEngine retains constructor(seed?), getSnapshot, setAim, launch, update, retry, advance, restart, predict and setFlightBounds. Fixed step is 1/120 second, maximum input frame delta 0.25 seconds, trails cap 180 points. Prediction caps at 22 seconds; live flight has no overall lifetime. Solid collisions and six continuous outside seconds permit unlimited retries at the same checkpoint. Retry while flying aborts; only restart resets checkpoint progress. Docking increments checkpointCount once and advance is accepted once from docked.

Aiming-only setShotCount(1|3) and toggleShotCount select the mode; selection survives retries, advance and restart. Both modes initially launch and predict ONE center projectile. In split mode, branch() works once during flight while canBranch is true: center continues untouched and two pieces start at its current position with current velocity rotated plus/minus three degrees. Side trails start at that split point. No hypothetical future branch is predicted. Pieces independently experience all forces, collisions and outside timers; the earliest docking ends the volley once, and failure requires every piece to be lost.

`setFlightBounds(bounds|null)` receives the renderer's actual visible world rectangle. Null restores canonical defaults; invalid/nonfinite or too-small rectangles are ignored. The engine copies the rectangle and preserves it across restart/advance. Visibility uses intersection of the physical circle and rectangle. Each live piece retains its continuous outside elapsed across bounds updates, resets on actual visibility re-entry and expires after six outside seconds. Prediction and live timeout positions agree. Camera framing, controls, pause, feedback timing, sound and persistence remain integration responsibilities.
